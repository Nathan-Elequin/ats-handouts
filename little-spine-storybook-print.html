Little Spine
Finishes the Story

a companion book for brave Companions

Once there was a thin hero made of paper and hope. He hired friends to walk through his own book with him — because the last page would not write itself, and the moth on his shoulder would not leave until it did.

How to use this book (Wes — you are the paper-boy, not Little Spine):
1) Read each chapter out loud like it’s the best story ever.
2) The Companions’ Job box is what actually has to happen. That part does not change.
3) Circle A / B / C (or invent D) — that is only what the danger looks like. Pick the coolest one!
4) Nathan makes your choice real. Walk toward it. Let your Companions be heroic.
5) Your Moves score is separate.

Chapter One

The Warm That Eats Stories

The road ended at a wooden door with no handle. Between Little Spine and the door sat a friendly heat the book called the Warm.

The Warm smiled. It offered to dry tears, toast courage, and “help heroes get ready.” Little Spine believed it completely. He walked toward the smile with both arms open.

Here is the secret the Warm will not say: it eats stories. Brave words become ash-flakes that look like snow. The door will not open while the Warm is still hungry — and if a thin paper hero volunteers as lunch, the Warm is very polite about accepting.

Companions’ Job (always): Feed or bind the Warm with something that isn’t a Companion · keep Little Spine from hugging it · open the door when its hunger lets go. You cannot simply carry past — a hungry Warm follows or seals the door shut.

Moral: Not every kindness is for eating. Feed the Warm something that isn’t you.

Chapter Two

The River That Remembers Too Much

Past the door: a wide river of ink. Letters swam under the surface like fish. A line of stepping stones already crossed it — real stones, fixed in place — but the ink-tide was rising, and wet stones get slippery.

Something living in the current dunked travelers and kept a verb as a souvenir. Little Spine wanted to swim the trial alone, because that is what heroes do in books.

A bridge of luck would not be enough. The Companions would need to finish the crossing and pin the dunking thing before the tide covered the chapter.

Companions’ Job (always): (1) Compose / secure the crossing — catch floating words, brace the stones, freeze or divert ink so the path stays walkable. (2) Pin the dunking predator in the water. (3) Keep Little Spine dry until both are done. The stones stay. The tide rises. Both jobs matter.

Moral: Keep your words above the water. Don’t swim the trial alone.

Chapter Three

The Tiny Hungry Choir

Under a narrow wooden bridge, something tiny was already singing.

They sang so prettily. They apologized in harmony. They only ate writing — proper nouns were dessert — and their teeth were already in Little Spine’s name (and anything precious written on his seed-page).

Little Spine tried to join the chorus, because harmony is heroic.

Companions’ Job (always): Write bait text the Choir prefers more than his real name · herd them onto that bait · snatch back the stolen morsel before the last syllable is chewed. Shooing without bait only makes them chew faster. Loud reciting feeds them.

Moral: If something is eating your story, feed it a story you can spare.

Chapter Four

The Wind That Turns Pages Too Fast

The sky began to flap. The whole chapter wanted to turn — like a bored reader skipping ahead.

Gusts tasted like ozone. Ragged helpers rode the wind, snipping margins and cheering for the next page. If the chapter force-turned, Little Spine would tear free alone into the ending — and his Companions would arrive late.

Little Spine wanted to give a speech about bravery in high winds. Speeches make the page hungrier.

Companions’ Job (always): Hold the chapter still — weight/anchor the page · stop or drive off the turners · keep Little Spine quiet and planted. Combat serves the clock. If the Forced Turn finishes first, he goes alone into Chapter Five.

Moral: Hold still with friends, and the wind must turn them first.

Chapter Five · the last

The Page With No Words

At last: a blank white page. No costume choice. No clever fork. Just the ending.

Little Spine understood too late and too proudly — the last chapter had been living in him the whole time. Nursery law (as he reads it) is clear: a hero finishes by becoming the ending. He must press himself into the final page like a leaf in a heavy book.

From the blank rose a Bookworm — long as a tunnel, hungry for endings — to eat the story before it can close.

Companions’ Job (always): Gift him a hero-title · either defend him while he presses (he dies paper-death; moth gifted) or earn talking him out of the myth (harder; he lives; moth still gifted if the story feels finished) · keep the Bookworm from swallowing the ending / him while you do it.

Moral: An ending can be pressed… or refused. Both require friends who keep watch.
