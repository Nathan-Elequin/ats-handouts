Little Spine
Finishes the Story

a companion book for brave Companions

Once there was a thin hero made of paper and hope. He hired friends to walk through his own book with him — because the last page would not write itself, and the moth on his shoulder would not leave until it did.

How to use this book (Wes):
1) Read the chapter out loud like it’s the best story ever.
2) Circle what the peril looks like (A / B / C) — or invent D.
3) Nathan makes it real. Then walk toward it. Let your Companions be heroic.
4) Your Moves score is separate — short names you can also twist with the Scroll of Spelling (once per page).

Chapter One

The Warm That Eats Stories

On the first road Little Spine met a friendly heat with a dragon’s smile.

“Come close,” said the Warm. “I only want to dry your tears. Brave heroes always let kindness touch them.”

But the Warm eats stories. It turns brave words into ash-flakes that look like snow. The door ahead will not open while the Warm is still hungry — and Little Spine, who trusts the book, keeps walking toward the smile.

Moral: Not every kindness is for eating. Feed the Warm something that isn’t you.

Chapter Two

The River That Remembers Too Much

Then came a river that remembered every word that ever fell in.

Letters swam under the surface like fish. Stepping stones sank if you doubted. Something in the current wanted to dunk Little Spine and keep a verb forever — a souvenir of almost-drowning.

The Companions would need more than a bridge. They would need to compose a crossing from remembered sentences… and pin the thing that remembers too much… before the ink-tide rose over the chapter.

Moral: Keep your words above the water — or borrow someone else’s dry path. And don’t swim the trial alone.

Chapter Three

The Tiny Hungry Choir

Under a narrow bridge sang the prettiest tiny chorus.

They were so polite. They apologized in harmony. They only ate writing — proper nouns were dessert — and they already had their teeth in Little Spine’s name.

You cannot simply shoo a choir. You must write them something better to sing… herd them onto it… and snatch back what they stole before the last syllable is gone.

Moral: If something is eating your story, feed it a story you can spare.

Chapter Four

The Wind That Turns Pages Too Fast

Then the sky became a thumb, flicking the world like a bored reader.

Pages flapped. Gusts tasted like ozone. Ragged shapes rode the wind with scissors for manners. The chapter itself tried to skip ahead — and leave Little Spine behind as a torn margin.

Loud heroics only made the wind hungrier for the next page. The Companions would have to hold the chapter still… or break the turners… before the book force-turned without them.

Moral: Hold still with friends, and the wind must turn them first.

Chapter Five · the last

The Page With No Words

At last: a page with no words.

Little Spine understood too late and too proudly — the last chapter had been living in him the whole time. Nursery law is clear: a hero finishes by becoming the ending. He must press himself into the final page like a leaf in a heavy book.

From the blank white rose a Bookworm — long as a tunnel, hungry for endings — to eat the story before it could close.

The Companions may gift him a hero-title. They may try to talk him out of pressing. The Bookworm will not wait politely either way.

Moral: An ending can be pressed… or refused. Both require friends who keep watch.
