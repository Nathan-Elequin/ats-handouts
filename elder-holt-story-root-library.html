The Elder Holt

Humble World-Tree anchor · living children’s library · roots dig other worlds each dawn and return with pages

Table truth: Elsewhere they tell of a World Tree stitching realms. Here in the Feywild the Holt looks smaller — a grove that grows stories. Bark niches hold nursery books. Each morning the roots come back dewey-wet with fresh ink from far places. Finished tales bind clean. Unfinished ones stay soft, blank, or half-lettered.

Paper-boy’s book is one of these — a thin adventure chapbook the Holt wrote on a quiet day. He is the first and only Parchdryad (call him the paper-boy). Last chapter lives in him. He believes he must press himself into the final page like a flower; when he does, the party helps choose his hero-title, he writes it, and the paper-boy ends — moth to the Companions.

Holt-root catch · Summer 2021

The Friends Who Would Not Become Stars

Look: Deep indigo cloth cover dusted with silver foil constellations that flake if you rub them. Title in a tall, spindly night-serif (like candle smoke letters). Front art: six tiny student silhouettes holding hands under a crack in a school floor that spills glittery dust upward. Endpapers are star-charts drawn wrong on purpose.

Back: Once there was a school for brave beginners. Six friends found a secret door under the stairs and a mean old magic that wanted to turn them into stardust for a scrapbook in the sky. This is the story of homework, hiding, and how friends can un-curse a constellation if they stay together until morning.

Canon: Weave of the Ninestone (Complete)

Holt-root catch · Winter 2024

The Children Who Walked Toward the Quiet Dark

Look: Matte charcoal board cover; title in bone-white hand lettering, slightly crooked like a child’s chalk. A single violet umbrella opens over five travelers on a road of ash. No shine except one umbral petal stamped in soft purple. Feels cold even in summer.

Back: A soft purple night began spreading like spilled juice, and monsters grew where the juice touched. Five tired travelers walked into the quiet dark looking for a place where new life could still grow. This is a bedtime story about being scared together, and about planting a seed where everyone said nothing could grow.

Canon: The Umbral Harbingers (Complete)

Holt-root catch · 2024–2025

The Thieves Who Would Not Clap for Gold

Look: Gilded paperback that looks expensive and slightly sticky. Loud carnival typeface in black and coin-yellow. Cover art: a smiling coin-king sitting on a pile of shiny things while five small figures peek from alley shadows. Foil coins peel off if you pick at them (the Holt hates that).

Back: In a city where money bought the rules, a golden bully made everyone clap. Five plucky troublemakers decided clapping was not the same as kindness. There are cards, airships, bad luck, and a very important lesson: gold is heavy, and friendship is lighter to carry when you run.

Canon: The Capital Offense (Complete)

Holt-root catch · Fall 2025

The Band That Made the Festival Tell the Truth

Look: Soft-touch neon paperback, rose-pink and stage-black. Bubble-letter title with one string of a guitar drawn as a glowing vine. Cover: six mismatched musicians mid-jump under paper lanterns; a sly music-mogul silhouette steals notes from the sky like cookies. Sparkle varnish only on the notes.

Back: There once was a sparkly festival that clapped too loud. A mean music-boss stole songs so nobody else could feel brave. A band of misfits played anyway — louder, weirder, truer — until the stages told the truth. This is a sing-along book. You are allowed to dance while reading.

Canon: Notorious (Complete)

Holt-root catch · Fall 2025

The Pilgrims Who Changed Before the Summit

Look: Soft watercolor boards that shift tint as you tilt them — spring green, summer gold, autumn rust, winter blue. Title in gentle rounded letters that look hand-repainted over older words. Cover: small pilgrims on a mountain path, each casting a second shadow in a different shape (stone, breeze, ripple, flame). A quiet figure with a changing cloak stands at the summit like a librarian of seasons. No battle poses — only footsteps mid-step.

Back: High on a sacred mountain, the big weather-gods keep a fragile peace — and once in a long while they invite (or nudge, or shove) pure-hearted travelers to climb.

The mountain does not only ask if you are brave. It asks if you can change. Can you become the shape a season needs? Can you let go of the you who started walking?

A picture-book of earth and air and water and fire… and the hardest trial of all: turning into someone new before you reach the top.

Canon: Trials of The Elements — Seasons of Change / Bestower of Change; teaser jacket only

Holt-root catch · Spring 2026 · FINISHED

The Racers Who Chased the Wrong Finish

Look: Glossy racing-stripe boards in emerald, sapphire, and checkered cream. Title in bold rounded sport-poster letters; the word Wrong is printed in soft black-jade foil that drinks the light. Cover art: a rattly cart full of mismatched friends streaking past jewel-kingdoms, pockets jingling with odd humming doodads. Endpapers: maps that look like race courses if you squint — and like treasure hunts if you don’t.

Back: Once there was a Great Race of crystal carts through jewel-lands and forgetful green mazes. The hurry-friends thought they were collecting trophies — shiny prizes for going fast. But the doodads in their pockets hummed with a secret job, and the finish line kept changing its mind. This is a picture-book about zooming, gathering, and discovering what a race is really for.

Bookmarks · pressed pages (real table ink)

Four roughly equidistant moments from prior sessions — one quote each from Kai, Drii, Ennalyn, and Edmund. Parentheticals are the Holt’s bored librarian voice (no full context). Quotes from session transcripts (light ASR cleanup only).

Each slip is stamped with the same mysterious mark: a tiny round sun at the center, and around it a spray of jagged angles — sharp flecks of light that spiral upward as they thin, like rays that learned how to flicker.

Bookmark I · early · Kai

(it seems here that a fox-eared girl has been told to speak her name into a shimmering wall between worlds, and after a nervous breath she answers aloud):

“I am Joy Incarnate. I am sun and laughter. I am Kai.”

(the wall rather greedily grabs her, and she vanishes through as if the page itself were hungry)

Bookmark II · early–middle · Drii

(it seems the moth-winged changeling has had enough of watching two lovers throw themselves at danger; he looks straight at the singer and says):

“So, next time you’re putting your boyfriend on a leash, right? If that’s what it takes. I don’t understand why you’re constantly martyring yourselves. Why you both would have no regard for your own safety.”

(nobody has a tidy answer, and the walk continues with rather more sulking than usual)

Bookmark III · middle–late · Ennalyn

(it seems two lovers are worrying aloud about what crossing into fairy-land will cost them, and after a quiet pause the singer answers):

“I don’t know what we’re going to have to give up. But I refuse to give up you.”

(he says he feels the same, and they keep walking toward the veil)

Bookmark IV · late · Edmund

(it seems the young man with the journals is being offered one of his own lost moth-pieces back, and warned it may hurt; after a pause he takes it anyway and says):

“I don’t remember it causing me much pain back when I had it to begin with. I would love to prove to her that we are capable of holding more than just one Lann — or one trait.”

(he starts looking around for the little lights that remember what was taken)
