Entering the Elder Holt

Read-aloud / walk-in · As They Seem · before the glowing chapbook · GM may trim mid-sentence

The Summer Court’s polite woods thin out the way conversation does when everyone realizes they’ve been overheard. Birdsong stops arguing with itself. The path stops pretending to be a path and becomes a suggestion of one — moss that remembers footprints, roots that have learned the habit of stairs.

Ahead, the trees are older than titles. Their bark is stacked in shelves. Between the shelves: niches the size of storybooks, some empty, some holding volumes that breathe when the wind turns a page for them. Sap beads on the wood like drying ink. If you listen the wrong way, you can hear binding thread being pulled taut somewhere underground.

This is the Elder Holt — not the World Tree the far continents brag about, and not the Noveltree that keeps courtly ledgers under glass. This is the humble cousin: a living archive that grew stories the way other groves grow fruit. Each dawn its roots dig other worlds — Chrysallis, old wars, festivals that already ended — and come back dewey-wet with fresh pages. Finished tales bind clean. Unfinished ones stay soft at the edges, blank as a held breath.

You smell rain that never quite falls: dewey-rain, the Holt’s own weather, the kind that softens paper without tearing it — unless something hungrier than weather has gotten into a book. Somewhere a nursery rhyme tries to finish itself and fails, looping the last line like a stuck latch.

Deeper in, a clearing opens around a stump worn smooth into a lectern. Upon it rests a children’s book too large for any ordinary child, glowing from within as if someone left a lantern between the pages. Prism cracks crawl the cover. Ozone pricks the tongue before each soft tick — light fracturing, a moth-shaped absence trembling on a paper shoulder nearby.

The Holt does not announce itself. It simply continues filing the world. And tonight, one thin volume on the stump is still writing.

Daggerheart-style asks (pick 1–2)

  • Who among you feels most watched by the empty niches — as if a book is waiting for your name?
  • What sound do you follow first: the binding-thread underground, the looping rhyme, or the ozone tick?
  • If your Lanns were moths of leaf and light, what would they do in a place that shelves stories?

Worldbuilding elicited (for you — don’t lecture)

  1. Humble World-Tree analogue — Feywild’s small cousin to the continent-spanning World Tree myth; grows stories, not just wood.
  2. Story-root library — bark niches hold nursery volumes; books can breathe / be turned by wind.
  3. Roots dig other worlds at dawn — return dewey-wet with ink from Chrysallis and elsewhere.
  4. Finished vs wet ink — complete campaigns bind; unfinished stay soft/blank (ATS still wet).
  5. Noveltree contrast — court library under glass vs wild Holt that grew roots through stories.
  6. Dewey-rain — Holt weather that softens paper; the rain during which a seed once fell into a book.
  7. Illuminated pages as doors — when woven light shatters a living story, pages become walkable (tree-stride for books).
  8. Song & memory navigation — oldest wood; you find your way by sound as much as sight (casting-call DNA).
