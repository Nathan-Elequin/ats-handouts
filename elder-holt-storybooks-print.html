Elder Holt · Storybook handouts (player-facing)

  1. Print Letter, Actual size / 100%, color on. Hide this strip automatically when printing.
  2. Finished books (pages 1–3): two handouts per sheet. Cut the outer dashed rectangle; fold on the vertical mid-line so Front faces out / Back faces out.
  3. ATS wet book (pages 4–5): larger. Page 4 = cover front + back (fold across middle). Page 5 = inside passages / bookmarks — keep with the cover or tuck inside when folded.
  4. No campaign names on the cards — only what the Holt printed for children.

Back

High on a sacred mountain, the big weather-gods keep a fragile peace — and once in a long while they invite (or nudge, or shove) pure-hearted travelers to climb. The mountain does not only ask if you are brave. It asks if you can change. Can you become the shape a season needs? Can you let go of the you who started walking? Back

Once there were four friends who crossed into a land where names blow away on the breeze and feelings wear funny costumes. Little moth-pieces of who they are keep fluttering off — and turning up in the oddest places. This is a story still being written about finding yourself after you’ve been scattered.

Inside the unfinished boards, four thin slips are pressed like bookmarks. Each bears the same mysterious mark: a tiny round sun at the center, and around it a spray of jagged angles — sharp flecks of light that spiral upward as they thin, like rays that learned how to flicker.

— open to the pressed pages —

Pressed pages

You fan the wet book open. The unfinished story doesn’t run page-to-page the way a finished one would — instead, four stamped slips wait in the gutter. You draw the first. Then the next. Each time you turn a slip, the ink seems to speak a little louder than a children’s book should.

First slip

You flip to the earliest-looking page. It seems here that a fox-eared girl has been told to speak her name into a shimmering wall between worlds, and after a nervous breath she answers aloud:

“I am Joy Incarnate. I am sun and laughter. I am Kai.”

The wall rather greedily grabs her, and she vanishes through as if the page itself were hungry. You turn the slip.

Second slip

The next bookmark is a little further in. It seems the moth-winged changeling has had enough of watching two lovers throw themselves at danger; he looks straight at the singer and says:

“So, next time you’re putting your boyfriend on a leash, right? If that’s what it takes. I don’t understand why you’re constantly martyring yourselves. Why you both would have no regard for your own safety.”

Nobody has a tidy answer, and the walk continues with rather more sulking than usual. You turn again.

Third slip

Deeper still. It seems two lovers are worrying aloud about what crossing into fairy-land will cost them, and after a quiet pause the singer answers:

“I don’t know what we’re going to have to give up. But I refuse to give up you.”

He says he feels the same, and they keep walking toward the veil. One slip left.

Fourth slip

Near the unfinished end. It seems the young man with the journals is being offered one of his own lost moth-pieces back, and warned it may hurt; after a pause he takes it anyway and says:

“I don’t remember it causing me much pain back when I had it to begin with. I would love to prove to her that we are capable of holding more than just one Lann — or one trait.”

He starts looking around for the little lights that remember what was taken. After that, the wet book simply… stops. The roots have not finished writing.
