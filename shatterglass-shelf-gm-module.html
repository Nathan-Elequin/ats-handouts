1. Premise & causality

In the Elder Holt (living archive), a weak seed sprouted inside a children’s chapbook and became a Parchdryad — bonded “tree” = that manuscript. Paper skin, ink-blood, readable body-text. Then the Lann Woven Light Shattering landed. Woven light raced his page-fibers and shattered, cracking the seal between reading a story and walking one. He hires the party as Companions to finish Page Five inside the book. Moth is theirs when the story ends. If he shatters, moth dies with him.

1b. Map placement — Elder Holt · why enter the book

Outer map (real Feywild): The Elder Holt — already on your casting map as “living archive / oldest wood / navigate by sound / song & memory.” Perfect host for a chapbook that woke up wrong.

What the party sees outside: a clearing of archive-trees; a too-large nursery chapbook propped like a lectern, glowing from within; prism cracks crawling the cover; ozone before each soft tick as light-fractures spread; the Parchdryad fretting beside it with a moth on his shoulder.

Inner “dungeon” (not on the regional map): the five pages made spatial. Fire/water/bugs/wind don’t need a glacier province — they are story-dangers inside Little Spine’s chapbook. Whimsical fey rule: when woven light shatters a living story, the illuminated pages become doors. Stepping into a glowing page works like a dryad’s tree-stride — but for books.

Session geography: tip → travel to Elder Holt → meet him at the glowing chapbook → he begs Companions to enter with him → cross the page-threshold → run Beats 1–5 inside → exit when the book closes (rite done / shatter / off-ramp).

Why this justifies the 5 rooms: you’re not inventing a new region that fights the map. You’re using a defined archive-holt as the lobby, and a classic fey trick (places that are stories) as the dungeon. Noveltree/Night Shelf stays court-library; Elder Holt is the wilder “books that grew roots” cousin.

1c. What “Woven Light Shattering” actually did

Keep two different “weaves” straight at the table:

WordMeansDo not confuse with
Page-fiber / parchment weaveHis body — dryad grown from book pulpThe Lann’s name
Woven lightThe Lann itself — memory & magic spun into luminous threadOrdinary paper
Causal chain (say this if anyone asks):
1. He was already a Parchdryad (seed in chapbook) — hollow, looping Page One.
2. The moth is woven light: Made of Memory and Magic braided until it shines.
3. It landed and that light threaded into his page-fibers, then shattered — prism bursts in the ink (kaleidoscope mosaic “in the blood”), a static taste when a filament snaps, a distant crack like ice as each light-strand breaks.
4. Shattered woven light doesn’t go out cleanly; it sticks in the text as illumination, overcharging the story until the pages can be entered.
5. Inside, “Shatterglass” is what broken woven light looks like when a story hardens into a place — laminated glare, frozen prism, ozone before the next filament pops.

So: paper-weave = species body. Woven Light Shattering = the event that lit him up, cracked him toward protagonist, and opened the book as a dungeon. The escort exists because a dryad alone cannot finish a task; the moth made the unfinished task walkable.

3. The children’s book — verify this

Bedside allegory. Not a dungeon map in prose — a 1:1 metaphor layer. Each page clues one beat. Wes’s body-text matches these pages; he will quote them aloud as “the Legend.” Print the five pages as a tiny handout or keep GM-only and let him “remember” them.

Little Spine Finishes the Story A Fey Nursery Chapbook · five soft pages · “for children who want endings”

Page One — The Warm That Eats Stories

Little Spine left the shelf because the elders said the ending was too sharp for soft paper.

On the first road he met a friendly stove with a dragon’s smile. “Come close,” said the Warm. “I only want to dry your tears.”

But the Warm eats stories. It turns brave words into ash-flakes that look like snow.

Little Spine learned: the first danger smiles and offers to help.

Clue → Beat 1: a welcoming heat that “helps” by destroying paper.

1 / 5

Page Two — The River That Remembers Too Much

Next came a river made of yesterday’s rain. It promised to show Little Spine every word he had ever been.

When he stepped in, the letters on his arms began to run. The river kept them. It loved memory so much it would not give any back.

Little Spine learned: to cross, you must keep your words above the water — or borrow someone else’s dry path.

Clue → Beat 2: water / thaw that dissolves ink; puzzle crossing.

2 / 5

Page Three — The Tiny Hungry Choir

Under a quiet bridge lived a choir no louder than crumbs. They sang the prettiest song Little Spine had ever heard.

They sang while they ate his verbs. They apologized between mouthfuls. “We are so small,” they hummed. “Surely you can spare a sentence.”

Little Spine learned: polite hunger still leaves holes in the page.

Clue → Beat 3: vermin / bookworms; setback that steals text.

3 / 5

Page Four — The Wind That Turns Pages Too Fast

Then the sky became a thumb. It flicked the world like a book it was bored with.

Knights of torn edges rode the gusts. They did not hate Little Spine. They only wanted the story to hurry.

Little Spine learned: if you stand alone in a hurry, you tear. Hold still with friends, and the wind must turn them first.

Clue → Beat 4: physical / mini-combat; shredding wind + foes.

4 / 5

Page Five — The Page With No Words

At the heart of the cracking light, Little Spine found the last page of his own story.

It was blank.

He cried, because blank felt like failing. Then he understood: an ending is not found. An ending is written — even a small one — while someone keeps the warm and the wet and the hungry and the hurry away.

He wrote one true line. The moth of leaves lifted like a bookmark removed. The book, at last, could close.

Clue → Beat 5: ritual = write on the blank; Companions hold the line; blank-scroll truth.

4. How to run the escort (lightweight)

Inspired by Johnn Four’s 5-room dungeon pacing, OSR pamphlet room keys (read-aloud → boldable facts → fail-forward), and escort best-practice: predictable NPC behavior, telegraphed fail state, threats that target the charge so the party plays defense.

Shatter Line (public) Track 0–6 on the table (tokens / dice). Starts at 0. At 6 he shatters — Lann deleted. Telegraph hard at 5.
His AI (say this once) He always walks toward the next “page.” He narrates the cool version. He fails heroic assists often. He will not stay behind cover without a Companion physically stopping him.
When Shatter Line rises Botched heroics · room threat hits him · seed-page lost/wet/burned · Fear spend · invented frailty exploited.
When it falls Brace / dry / warm carefully / rewrite a stolen sentence onto him / successful protect beat.
Division of labor: Wes wants out loud, reads each page excited, and picks CYOA flavor (A/B/C/invent) for each peril — his pick becomes the fiction. You keep the pillar identical (heat / wet / hungry-text / tear-hurry / blank ending), run Shatter Line, Fear, telegraphs, rulings. Secret score = his menu. You improv the skin.

Session hook: tip toward the Elder Holt (living archive / cracking light in an old chapbook) → meet him at the glowing lectern-book → deal stated → step through an illuminated page into Beats 1–5.

6. Five beats — room keys

Format: read-feel → facts → threat to him → play loop → fail-forward. Varied pillars as requested.

1 · The Warm That Eats Stories Entrance · Hazard / soft guardian FIRE Page 1
A curtain of friendly amber heat shimmers between two leaning ice-shelves. Ash falls upward. Something in the warmth chuckles like a stove that learned manners.

Landmark: Heat curtain (safe for most PCs; lethal-cute to paper). Ember-wisps (3–5) drift like helpful lanterns. A brass kettle sits on a rock, whistling a welcome.

Threat to him: Any wisp that “dries his tears” chars margin-text. Standing in the curtain = Shatter Line +1 and a visible scorch quote.

Play: Not a boss fight. Trap/hazard + soft guardian. Wisps offer help. If ignored, they insist. Extinguish with cold / smother / redirect into ice (hiss comedy). Or negotiate: give the Warm a scrap of unimportant writing to eat instead.

If he quotes Page 1: he will walk toward the kettle smiling.

Fail-forward: He gets lightly toasted (+1 Line) but a scorch-mark forms a usable rune-clue for Beat 2 (“keep words above water”).

Exit: Curtain parts when heat is fed, smothered, or bravely crossed with him wrapped/shielded.

2 · The River That Remembers Too Much Puzzle / physical skill WATER Page 2
A melt-channel cuts the shelf — clear, cold, humming with stolen sentences. Letters ghost by under the surface like fish. The far bank shows prism veins bright as stained glass.

Landmark: Ford 15–20 ft. Stepping stones that sink if you doubt (light Gravity Cliff echo — optional). Rope-ice. A dry arch of laminated page-ice upstream (cracked, Skill to traverse).

Threat to him: Water runs his ink. Full dunk = Shatter Line +2 and he temporarily loses a verb (cannot declare Heroic Assist until a Companion “lends a word”).

Play: Puzzle / skill. Multiple solutions: bridge from gear · carry him · freeze a path · have him Tree-Meld into a companion’s wooden shield/staff for one crossing · step-stones timed to ozone-cracks. Brute swimming works for PCs, bad for him.

Clue from book: “keep your words above the water — or borrow someone else’s dry path.”

Fail-forward: Partial dunk steals one body-sentence; it appears floating in the river. Retrieve it later in Beat 3 (Choir stole it too) or rewrite.

3 · The Tiny Hungry Choir Trick / setback BUGS Page 3
A narrow ice-spine spans a dark cleft. From below: the prettiest tiny chorus. Silver-glints. The song is an apology in harmony.

Landmark: Bridge. Under-cleft nests of Margin Moths / bookworms / glass-silverfish (pick one aesthetic). They only eat writing — not raw wood, not people.

Threat to him: They adore him. Swarm = polite munching of proper nouns. Shatter Line +1 per round he stands unshielded on the bridge. Seed-page is dessert.

Play: Setback / trick — not a slaughter. Distract with bait text (write decoy sentences on ice). Smoke. Soft music that out-apologizes them. Coat him in non-written cloak. If they fight, insects scatter but steal one key word from his chest (“brave” / his invented name).

Twist: After “victory,” they leave a thank-you note made of his own eaten verbs — a holey poem. Use holes as a cipher for Beat 5 (optional).

Fail-forward: Name stolen → he can’t introduce himself until renamed by a Companion (cute foil beat for Drii/Edmund).

4 · The Wind That Turns Pages Too Fast Mini-combat + physical TEAR / COLD / STATIC Page 4
The shelf becomes a corridor of flapping ice-pages. Wind turns them too fast. Ozone bites the tongue. Ragged shapes ride the gusts — edges like scissors, manners like bored librarians.

Landmark: Long gallery. Gust cycles every few rounds (telegraph: static taste → crack → blast). Cover niches. Prism veins = handholds.

Foes: 2–4 Page-Turn Knights (shredding wind-fey / living paper-cuts). They aggro the loudest story — usually him. They deal scratch damage to PCs; to him they threaten tearing (Line).

Threat to him: Unbraced gust = Line +1. Knight hit = Line +1 and a literal flap of skin-page tears (bandage = rewrite).

Play: Mini-combat + physical skill. Hold him in a niche · form a windbreak · ground the static (metal/earth) · use his failed Tree Stride as a decoy (he pops halfway into a dead trunk and gets stuck — extract mid-fight).

Book clue: “Hold still with friends, and the wind must turn them first.” Tanking for him is the intended read.

Fail-forward: He is blown to The Page With No Words early, alone, for 1 beat — panic clock — party catches up into Ritual under worse terms.

5 · The Page With No Words Ritual · tower defense · revelation BLANK PAGE · PRESS-IN Page 5
The shelf opens into a hollow where ice-veins braid into a stained-glass circulatory system. At center: a low dais of clear ice — an empty final page standing upright like a pane. Above it, a moth-shaped absence in the light. He realizes, late and proud: the last chapter has been living in him the whole time.

See §7 Final ritual. Runner: GM Console. One-screen map: elder-holt-dungeon-map.html.

7. Final ritual — press-in (locked)

What he believes: A hero finishes the book by becoming the ending — he must press himself into the final page like pressed leaves (nursery myth he treats as law).

What is true: The last chapter is in him. Pressing = paper-death + purpose fulfilled → he willingly gifts the Lann. Talking him out of pressing = earned off-ramp only.

Naming: Wes = paper-boy / Parchdryad escort. Little Spine = chapbook hero (not Wes). At the press he wants the party to gift a hero-title; if they freeze, he invents one.

Procedure (run as clock)

  1. Reveal at the Heart: last chapter lives in him; the upright blank page is waiting to receive a pressed hero.
  2. Title: Party gifts a hero-title (or he self-names). Soft seeds: Spine Who Finished · Little Page Brave · Companion of Companions.
  3. Press clock: 3 beats of committing / aligning / pressing while Companions run tower defense.
  4. Waves: leftover wisps · Choir reprise · a Knight · a crack under the dais · Fear spend. Threats prefer him. If Line hits 6 mid-rite → shatter.
  5. Press succeeds: He becomes the page (dies). Lann moth lifts to Drii / party as agreed.
  6. Off-ramp (earned): Someone (esp. Drii) convinces the living unfinished book / pre-Legend sapling was already enough — he gifts without pressing.
  7. Exit ritual: Runs Before Rain pithy lesson → snap → Wes deadpan → leaves table.

Why this finalizes the journey

  • Dryad / book logic: pressed leaf = finished specimen; he was always pulp waiting for a last chapter.
  • Lann logic: Woven Light’s shatter finishes when the story can close; moth’s work is done.
  • Escort logic: party win was never “clear rooms” — it was keep the manuscript alive long enough to choose how it ends.
  • CYOA note: Rooms 1–4 are Wes’s costume picks; Room 5 has no skin menu — only title + press / talk-out.
